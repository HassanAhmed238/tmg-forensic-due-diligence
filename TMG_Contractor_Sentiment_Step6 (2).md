**Research cut-off:** 12 October 2026  
**Geography:** Egypt, with explicit separation of Saudi Arabia references where relevant.  
**Purpose:** Assess public evidence of contractors', subcontractors', suppliers' and consultants' experiences with Talaat Moustafa Group (TMG), related entities, and parties in its contracting chain.

## Executive conclusion

The targeted payment-focused deep search identified **two relevant contractor/subcontractor dues reports**, but they differ sharply in recency and attribution. In October 2008, a named Madinaty contractor alleged that Alexandria Construction Company withheld his dues, leaving him unable to pay his workers; a company manager was quoted acknowledging that salary disbursement had been deliberately delayed in the then-existing circumstances, while saying arrears were being paid. In November 2024, Mada Masr reported an allegation that local people who performed subcontracting work on SouthMED had not been paid because the Armed Forces had delayed payments to primary contractors. That report does **not** establish that TMG itself owed or withheld those payments.

The public record found here is **not enough to prove a current, recurring TMG policy** of delaying or withholding contractors' money. It is also not enough to conclude that TMG always pays on time. No reviewed source supplied a verified certificate register, contract due dates, bank payment dates, or a final judgment resolving the dues claims. Workforce complaints from Alexandria Construction employees in 2024–2025 are relevant to employee remuneration and working conditions, but they must not be presented as evidence of unpaid subcontractor invoices.

This is a finding about the limits of publicly discoverable evidence, not proof that other disputes do not occur. A critical attribution rule applies: TMG as developer, its affiliated construction companies, external main contractors, public-sector counterparties and subcontractors are different actors. A complaint must be traced to the entity that signed the contract and owed the relevant amount.

### Payment-risk conclusion after the targeted search

- **Yes, public complaints exist.** The most direct case is the named 2008 Madinaty contractor's allegation concerning dues owed by Alexandria Construction Company. The company-side explanation reported at the time described deliberately delayed wage disbursement and said arrears were being paid.
- **There is also a relevant 2024 SouthMED subcontracting allegation**, but the reported cause was upstream payment delay attributed by an anonymous local source to the Armed Forces' payments to primary contractors. It must not be represented as proof that TMG directly delayed or withheld the subcontractors' money.
- **The recent evidence remains incomplete.** I did not find a second, independently corroborated recent case naming a TMG entity as the direct debtor, or documentary proof of current overdue IPCs, retention, final accounts or disputed deductions.
- **Do not count labor protests as contractor-payment cases.** The 2024–2025 employee complaints are context about pay and working conditions within an affiliated construction entity, not evidence about payment terms under third-party construction contracts.

## Findings by issue

### 1. Payment of interim certificates, outstanding dues and retention

No independently corroborated public testimony was identified in the reviewed results that names TMG or a clearly identified TMG contracting entity as the payer responsible for a specific overdue interim payment certificate, retention balance, advance-payment recovery dispute or final account. No certificate number, amount, contractual due date, actual payment date, acknowledgement of debt or court judgment was located in this public sweep.

Consequently, it would be unsound to label TMG a late payer or a consistently reliable payer on this evidence. The public record sampled is not granular enough to calculate days-to-payment or payment-term compliance.

### 2. Repeat business and positive professional relationships

Several public records are consistent with repeat relationships: Concrete Plus publicly described work on two Noor City projects before its SouthMED award; an LLD professional post described a long-running TMG collaboration; and suppliers/contractors published project portfolio entries for East Hub Madinaty and Noor City. These are meaningful commercial-relationship indicators, but public announcements are selective and do not replace reference checks, payment ledgers or signed close-out records.

### 3. Tendering, variations, deductions, approvals and final accounts

No TMG-specific, independently corroborated public account was found in this search sweep that reliably documents bid fairness, change-order approval timelines, disputed measured quantities, back-charges, liquidated damages, termination, or final-account settlement. These categories remain **unverified**, rather than positive or negative.

### 4. Attribution risk

Public East Hub materials list several parties with different scopes, including Atrium Quality Contractors, Alexandria Construction Company, SQUARE Engineering Firm, MCBS, Siraj Lighting, design consultants and the project manager. If an adverse experience is later found, the investigation must identify which entity signed the relevant subcontract or purchase order, which entity certified the work, and which entity owed payment. A site-level dispute with an external main contractor should not be relabelled as a direct TMG dispute without evidence.

## What the current evidence supports — and does not support

**Supported:** TMG and its project ecosystem engage named main contractors, in-group construction/MEP entities, suppliers and specialist design providers on large projects. Public announcements include repeat relationships and positive project statements.

**Not supported by the evidence collected:** a claim that TMG has a recurring late-payment problem; the opposite claim that it pays all subcontractors on time; a quantitative payment-performance ranking; or conclusions about dispute frequency across all Egypt projects. Two payment-related allegations are now recorded above, but only the 2008 report identifies a named contractor and a TMG-affiliated construction entity as the direct counterparty; the 2024 SouthMED report attributes the upstream delay to another party and relies on an anonymous source. Neither allegation was independently resolved through payment records or a final judgment in this review.

No copies of the same announcement or Arabic/English versions of the same report are counted as separate independent cases. Employee wage complaints are not counted as subcontractor-payment cases.

## Priority due-diligence follow-up

The highest-value next step is documentary/reference diligence rather than additional inference from promotional posts.

1. **Concrete Plus:** request two completed Noor City project references and a SouthMED commercial reference; verify certificate turnaround, payment timing, variation approval, retention release, and repeat-work rationale.
2. **Siraj Lighting and EMAS El Sewedy:** seek project-specific supplier references and, with permission, redacted purchase orders, invoices, delivery/acceptance records, and paid-date evidence.
3. **Atrium Quality Contractors, Alexandria Construction Company and MCBS:** clarify the specific legal entity that contracted each scope and whether any downstream subcontractor payment disputes were recorded.
4. **Project consultants and commercial managers:** request non-confidential references for East Hub, Noor City and SouthMED on certification workflows, scope changes, technical approvals, deductions and final accounts.
5. **Independent legal/document checks:** search case and arbitration records by the exact legal names of the contracting entity and counterparty, not only the TMG brand; confirm the case number, parties, subject, current procedural status and judgment/order before characterizing any dispute.
6. **Structured interviews:** speak separately to current and former commercial managers, quantity surveyors, procurement staff, supplier credit controllers and subcontractor finance leads across civil/structural, MEP, infrastructure, finishing, landscaping and specialist systems. Ask for project, contract entity, period, amount bracket, due date, actual paid date and documentary support. Obtain consent before using names or quotations.

## Suggested evidence request checklist

For each reference, request: signed contract/subcontract or purchase order; payment clauses and certification calendar; anonymized IPC register (submission, approval, due date, paid date); retention and advance-payment ledger; variation/claim log; deduction/penalty register; final-account settlement statement; close-out certificate; and permission to contact the counterparty's finance/commercial representative.

Only calculate payment days from documented dates. Distinguish approval delay from payment delay, disputed from undisputed amounts, and TMG-direct contracts from upstream/downstream chains. Treat repeat awards as an indicator worth investigating, not proof that all previous payments were timely.

## C. Sentiment review across the expanded network

### Negative payment evidence

1. **ACC / Madinaty (2008):** the strongest direct named contractor allegation against a TMG-associated construction entity, accompanied by a company-side response reported by the same newspaper. The two articles are one episode, not two independent cases.
2. **SouthMED local subcontracting work (reported 2024):** a relevant report of non-payment in the project chain, but the source was anonymous and attributed the delay to upstream payments by the Armed Forces to primary contractors. Neither the main contractor nor the subcontractor was named. This is not evidence that TMG or Concrete Plus directly withheld payment.
3. **Employee/wage protests at ACC (2024–2025):** documented concerns about wages, bonuses, layoffs and working conditions. These are relevant to employment practices but must not be counted as third-party subcontractor invoice disputes.

### Positive / continuity signals that do not prove payment reliability

1. **Concrete Plus repeat work:** public contract announcements and the CEO's references to prior Noor City projects show continuity of relationship. This merits reference checks but does not prove that earlier IPCs were paid on time.
2. **Everlast Construction / Open Air Mall:** a subcontractor publicly described participating in steel fabrication and erection under Atrium. The tone was positive, but there is no reference to commercial settlement.
3. **Knauf Egypt / Four Seasons Luxor:** a supplier's post praised technical collaboration and delivery solutions. It is promotional/project-partner content and does not establish payment timing.
4. **EMAS El Sewedy / Noor City and Siraj Lighting / East Hub:** public project participation/supply records establish the supply chain; no invoice settlement outcome is disclosed.
5. **Former Atrium QS profile:** a publicly accessible CV/profile describes processing subcontractor invoices efficiently and supporting timely payment. This is self-authored professional marketing, not testimony from a subcontractor or independent validation; confidence in it as a payment-performance signal is low.

### What was not found

The expanded public search did not identify a recent, independently corroborated case naming **AQC, the Atrium–CCC JV, CCC on the TMG packages, Concrete Plus, Everlast, Knauf, Devzah SI, Siraj Lighting or EMAS El Sewedy** as the direct party that withheld a specific subcontractor's certified dues on the identified TMG/Atrium projects. That is not proof that no such dispute exists. Private subcontract terms, supplier credit limits, disputed valuations and informal settlements are rarely exposed in search-indexed public content.

## D. Evidentiary safeguards and next steps

- Keep project participation, positive technical testimonial, employment complaint, and payment dispute as separate evidence types.
- For every lead, establish the exact legal entity on the subcontract or PO; a brand name or construction site is insufficient.
- Request, with consent, redacted contract/payment records: submission date, certification date, contractual due date, actual transfer date, retention/advance recovery, variations, deductions and final-account status.
- Ask separately whether any delay was in **measurement/approval** or in **cash payment after approval**.
- Treat a joint venture as a distinct contractual counterparty for the relevant package. Do not assume that TMG, AQC, APM and CCC have interchangeable payment obligations.
- Treat public project announcements as relationship evidence only; they must not be presented as proof of either timely payment or non-payment.

## Revised conclusion after contractor-network expansion

The expanded review identifies a broader set of publicly named counterparties across TMG/Atrium projects, including CCC (JV package contractor), Concrete Plus (SouthMED main contractor), Everlast Construction (specialist subcontractor), Devzah SI (CP05 subcontractor/vendor record), Knauf Egypt and EMAS El Sewedy (suppliers), and Siraj Lighting (project supplier/specialist). It also distinguishes project managers and consultants from contractors and distinguishes Atrium Quality Contractors from Atrium Project Management.

**Payment-risk conclusion is unchanged:** one clear historical direct allegation is documented against Alexandria Construction Company in 2008; a newer 2024 SouthMED report raises an upstream payment-chain concern but does not identify TMG or a named main contractor as the direct debtor. Publicly visible positive supplier/contractor posts and repeat awards are not proof of on-time payment. No sufficient public evidence was found to rate TMG/AQC as systematically late payers or as consistently reliable payers. The highest-value next step is reference calls plus redacted payment records from the named counterparties above.
