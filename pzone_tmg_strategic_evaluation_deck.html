The Executive Thesis

The Asymmetry
of Scale.

Megaprojects like SouthMED (23M sqm) and Noor (5,000 feddans) create an optical illusion of infinite liquidity. But the EGX audited books reveal that TMG operates with negative net cash (-EGP 13.38B) once sale-and-leaseback notes are consolidated.

In high-end water features and lagoons, 60% to 70% of P.Zone's costs are hard-currency MEP imports (pumps, filtration, geomembrane, controls). European vendors demand cash upfront or confirmed LCs. TMG pays with deferred notes payable.

Accepting a standard subcontract means P.Zone takes equity-grade downside risk with utility-grade capped margins. Unless the contract is structurally re-engineered, scale becomes fatal.

Subcontractor Exposure Analysis

Five Structural Killers

i.
Foreign Currency & Import Mismatch
Specialty pumps, filtration skids, PLC automations, and liner membranes must be bought in EUR/USD cash. Fixed-price EGP subcontracts lead to instant margin death upon currency fluctuation.
60–70% FX Share
ii.
The Deferred Notes Payable Trap
Subcontractors are routinely paid in post-dated checks (6 to 18 months). Discounting these checks at Egyptian banks costs 28.6% annually, completely wiping out net operating margins.
DPO > 180 Days
iii.
The Captive General Contractor Layer
P.Zone will not contract directly with TMGH, but with ACC (100% TM family) or tier-1s (Concrete Plus, Atrium). 'Pay-When-Paid' clauses freeze subcontractor funds during developer disputes.
Pay-When-Paid Risk
iv.
Civil Work Delays & Prolongation Costs
Lakes and pools require finished earthworks, deep excavation, and structural concrete. When main contractors delay civil milestones, P.Zone's overhead and mobilized teams bleed without compensation.
Schedule Drift
v.
Retention Snagging & Hydrostatic Testing
The 5% to 10% defects liability retention is easily withheld for years under minor water-tightness or landscaping snag list objections, serving as involuntary developer working capital.
5–10% Retention
